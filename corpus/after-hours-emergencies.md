# After-hours emergencies

## What counts as a dental emergency

A dental emergency is facial swelling, a knocked-out or broken tooth after an injury, bleeding that does not stop after 20 minutes of firm pressure, or severe pain that over-the-counter pain medication does not control. Lost fillings, loose crowns and chipped teeth without pain can wait for the next working day.

## When to send a patient to hospital

Send the patient straight to the nearest hospital emergency unit, without waiting for the on-call dentist, if the swelling affects breathing or swallowing, the swelling is closing the eye, there is a high fever with the swelling, or the jaw was injured in an accident.

## On-call rota

One dentist is on call each week, from Friday 17:00 to the following Friday 08:00. The practice manager prepares the rota every month and pins it on the staff room board. The after-hours line +27 00 000 0999 forwards automatically to the on-call dentist's phone.

## After-hours call-out fee

An after-hours consultation has a call-out fee of R950, plus the cost of any treatment, payable on the day. The call-out fee is waived when the problem is a complication of treatment done at the practice in the previous 14 days.

## Knocked-out adult tooth

Tell the caller to pick the tooth up by the crown, not the root, rinse it briefly in milk and push it back into the socket if they can. If not, keep it in milk and come to the practice within 60 minutes. Never replant a knocked-out baby tooth.
